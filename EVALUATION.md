# Architecture and evaluation

## Information flow

`game.py` owns the deck, dice, resource inventories, legal actions, and state transitions. AI policies receive an immutable player observation containing their own hand and development cards, board state, resource supply, other players' public card counts, and public transfers. The policy API keeps the authoritative game instance and its random seed private. GUI saves store a seed and action replay locally, so access to a save can reveal hidden information.

Coaching samples hidden inventories and development cards consistent with visible totals and known lower bounds. The lower bounds use public production, payments, trades, and the number of discarded or stolen cards. Unknown transfers and shuffled development cards retain uncertainty. Simulated players each receive their own observation. Rollouts compare alternative first actions using the same sampled worlds and random seeds, then follow the rollout policy to victory or the cutoff. The random streams can diverge when actions consume different numbers of draws.

Each displayed action has at least 1,000 full continuations. Estimates report wins divided by all attempted continuations and a 95% Wilson interval for wins by the cutoff. Unfinished games are shown separately. The interval covers simulation sampling variation under the assumed continuation policy. Differences between the rollout policy and real human play add uncertainty. The ordinary hint compares a shortlist. Select an action in the move list to include it in the next comparison, or use **Compare offer** to evaluate a draft trade. Hard matches use the loaded weights for opponents during coaching. Full coaching continuations approximate Hard play with the underlying policy. Standard and Easy matches use the Standard opponent model.

## Policy development

The Standard opponent weighs production, diversity, build affordability, expansion, and robber denial. Hard automatically loads weights selected through seeded match experiments. Candidate policies consider resource scarcity, production planning, routes, and opponent decisions during setup. The candidates compete as seat zero against three Standard opponents with independently randomized boards, order, decks, and dice. Training batches reuse seed sets across candidates for a controlled comparison. Holdouts use separate seed sets.

With endgame search, Hard won **410 of 1,000** holdout games against three Standard opponents (41.0%, 95% Wilson interval 38.0%–44.1%). The underlying policy won 394 of the same games. Search added 19 wins and lost three on paired seeds, so Hard loads it automatically. A second independent 1,000-game holdout measured Hard at 435 wins. On the separate shuffled-board holdout, the underlying policy won 396 of 1,000 games. The 70% win-rate target remains open. On the Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C test environment with a two-CPU quota, a three-action CPython hint took **95.51 s** during setup and **53.07 s** at the midgame test position. [PyPy comparison](artifacts/coaching-pypy.json) took 93.53 s and 35.46 s for the same cases. Skipping policy calls for forced moves and reducing repeated rollout work reproduced all seeded estimates. The optimized CPython run took **62.47 s** during setup and **40.66 s** at midgame on the same CPU quota. Sharing deterministic opening decisions across simulation prefixes brought the latest run to **48.74 s** during setup and **30.74 s** at midgame, with the same seeded estimates. The three-second hint target remains open.
