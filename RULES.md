# How to play

## Establish your network

Four players share an island of 19 hexes. Each productive hex shows a resource and a dice total. The first turn order is randomized. In that order, each player places one settlement and an adjacent road. Reverse the player order for the second settlement and road. The second settlement grants one card per adjacent productive hex. Any two buildings must have at least one empty intersection between them.

## Produce and act

On your turn, you may play one eligible development card before or after rolling. Roll two dice. Each settlement next to a matching unblocked hex receives one card, and each city receives two. Apply supply shortages by resource. If multiple players are owed more than the supply can pay, nobody receives that resource. If only one player is owed the resource, they receive as much as the supply can pay.

On a seven, each player with eight or more resource cards returns half their hand, rounding down. Move the robber to another hex, then steal one random resource card from an eligible opponent with a building on that hex. The robber suppresses its hex's production until moved. A knight moves the robber without causing discards.

After production or resolving a seven, build, trade, play an eligible development card, or end your turn. You can repeat construction and trading as long as the resources and pieces are available.

| Build | Cost | Placement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Road | 1 wood, 1 brick | Connect to your road or building. An opposing building prevents you from connecting through that intersection. |
| Settlement | 1 wood, 1 brick, 1 wool, 1 grain | Connect to your road and observe the distance rule. |
| City | 2 grain, 3 ore | Upgrade your settlement. The settlement returns to your supply. |
| Development card | 1 wool, 1 grain, 1 ore | Draw privately from the shared pile. |

Each player has 15 roads, five settlements, and four cities. Supply trades normally exchange four identical cards for one card of a different resource. A building on a general harbor unlocks 3:1 exchanges. A specialized harbor unlocks 2:1 exchanges in the harbor's resource. Players can propose bundles, accept, decline, or counter. Domestic trades always involve the active player, exchange resources on both sides, and exclude matching resources.

## Development cards and points

The 25-card deck contains 14 knights, five victory points, two road building, two plenty, and two monopoly cards. Play at most one eligible action card per turn. Newly purchased action cards become eligible on a later turn. Victory-point cards stay private until they take you to the victory threshold during your turn, including on the turn you purchase them.

- **Knight** moves the robber. The first player to play three knights earns the army award. A rival must play more knights than the holder to take it.
- **Road building** places up to two legal roads without spending resources.
- **Plenty** takes two available resource cards of your choice from the supply.
- **Monopoly** names one resource and collects every opponent's cards of that type.
- **Victory point** contributes one point.

A settlement is worth one point, a city two. A player claims the two-point route award for a continuous trail of at least five roads. Count each edge once and stop at opposing buildings. An existing award holder keeps it on a tie. If a route cut disqualifies the holder and eligible players tie for first place, remove the award until someone leads outright. The two-point army award follows the knight rule above. Win as soon as you have at least ten points on your own turn.
